**What happened.** CISA has discontinued six free cybersecurity assessments that critical infrastructure operators used to evaluate operational technology security, a cut attributed by Tatyana Bolton of the OT Cyber Coalition to severe budget reductions at the agency.

**Why it matters.** This ends the practice of treating a CISA assessment as an external check on OT security posture before connecting AI systems to those environments. Any AI governance program that assumed a federal review would catch OT security gaps was leaning on a backstop that no longer runs those checks. What remains is self-reported posture from vendors and internal teams, with no free federal verification behind it.

**What to do.** AI governance leads should treat every OT security claim feeding into an AI deployment decision as unverified until the operator has either commissioned a paid third-party assessment or documented an internal equivalent with the same scope as the discontinued CISA reviews. The question for your CTO: which of our AI-connected OT systems were last verified by a CISA assessment, and what have we booked to replace that check?

**Where I would be wrong.** Acting on this now means paying for assessments an operator may have judged unnecessary, and possibly slowing an AI-OT integration while a review is arranged. Waiting costs more if a gap in self-reported posture goes unverified and an AI system inherits an OT vulnerability nobody priced. The first error is a budget line. The second is an incident with no independent record to show what was checked and when.
