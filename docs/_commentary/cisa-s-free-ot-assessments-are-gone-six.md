**What happened.** CISA has discontinued six free cybersecurity assessments that critical infrastructure operators used to evaluate operational technology security. Tatyana Bolton, executive director of the OT Cyber Coalition, attributed the cuts to severe budget reductions. No replacement federal program has been named in the material available.

**Why it matters.** Any AI system your organization connects to OT was likely scoped against a security baseline that assumed periodic federal verification existed as a backstop. That assumption is now false. The baseline your AI governance program relies on is self-reported by the operator, checked by whatever paid assessment you choose to book, and nothing else.

**What to do.** Treat every AI system with an OT connection as running against an unverified baseline until you commission a paid assessment covering the same ground CISA's programs covered. The question for your CTO: which of our AI-to-OT integrations were last security-validated by a CISA assessment, and what is our plan to replace that validation now that it is gone?

**Where I would be wrong.** Acting on this costs money now: booking paid assessments for systems that may already be adequately covered by internal audits or vendor attestations. Waiting costs more if it's wrong: an AI system operating on a false assumption of federal-grade verification, inside an OT environment, discovered only after an incident. The paid assessment is the cheaper mistake to make.
