**What happened.** MISO has filed a FERC proposal that splits large loads above 50 megawatts into two regulatory classes, creating a new subcategory called computational loads. The filing defines who falls into the category. It does not yet attach specific reliability obligations to that category.

**Why it matters.** The assumption in most compliance planning is that a new FERC classification arrives with its rules attached. This filing separates the two steps: classification now, obligations later, through a process that has not been specified. Anyone tracking this proceeding as a rules announcement is tracking the wrong document.

**What to do.** Regulatory affairs teams should treat this filing as the moment to register as an interested party in the docket, not as the moment to assess compliance exposure. The question to put to your CTO: which of our facilities would fall into MISO's computational load category once obligations are attached, and do we have a channel to comment before those obligations are written?

**Where I would be wrong.** Acting now means spending staff time on a docket that FERC could reject or MISO could withdraw, at the cost of that attention elsewhere. Waiting means missing the window to shape definitions before they harden, and definitions set early are expensive to reopen once obligations attach to them. The filing itself gives no date for when obligations follow classification, so neither cost can be sized yet with confidence.
